# Pi optimize

Usage: `/skill:pi-optimize <max-trials>`, for example `/skill:pi-optimize 20`.
Explicit named lookup also works through `load_workflow` with name `pi-optimize` and kind `skill`; obtain the human's trial argument separately. Pi appends command arguments as user text, not shell substitutions.

## Governing tenets

Do not assume: verify critical facts and the intended action before launch. Even slight uncertainty about an execution-critical fact calls for a bounded check first; ask the human if it cannot be verified. Begin only when what to do is clear. Uncertainty about an experiment's outcome is legitimate and belongs in its hypothesis/falsifier, not a pretense of certainty or a reason to stall.

Apply KISS: choose the simplest approach meeting requirements without sacrificing correctness or safety. Existing meaningful configuration knobs are eligible; this does not authorize new knobs, abstractions, dependencies, or speculative features. Stay within the agreed MVP; only the human expands scope. Use TDD for authorized script/fix work, never as an excuse to expand the feature or rewrite fixed official benchmark tests.

When progress stalls or several approaches fail, research early enough to avoid churn: search the exact symptom, underlying mechanism, and broader problem, including existing solutions and others' reasoning. Treat results as suggested paths, not authority; verify promising ideas against the actual system and requirements. Never let research change the goal/scope, and never search secrets, private code, or internal data. This is an escape hatch, not a mandatory research round per trial. Guidance updates must not block an active benchmark or its next three-question decision.

## Top-of-session invariant register

At session start and resume, put the human-dictated invariant register at the top of the campaign runbook: each value, human source, effective override/precedence, and verification proof. Freeze model/provider/auth route, reasoning effort, human-fixed sampling/token limits, task/grader per comparison, resource/time budgets, required verification/permissions, baseline/fresh-state conditions, and total/sub-budgets unless the USER explicitly changes them. Never automatically tune reasoning effort; matching a comparator's effort is a protocol requirement, not a fairness label to waive later. Missing or ambiguous invariant values require asking the human before launch; do not invent defaults.

Keep a separate experiment-variable allowlist with explicit eligibility evidence. Every proposed knob/diff must be eligible and must not alter a human pin. A request to "optimize settings" is not authority to change human-fixed settings. Check actual CLI/settings precedence against the register before EVERY launch, including each concurrent attempt, even when reusing infrastructure. After each run verify recorded actual values against it.

If any invariant is violated, stop further launches, preserve the grade/raw evidence and spent budget, and mark the attempt protocol-invalid, excluded from winner comparisons. There is no refund, silent exploratory relabeling, promotion, or automatic rerun. Correct the ledger/report explicitly and obtain the human's direction on resumption. Fast iteration never bypasses this check.

## Establish authority and create the campaign record

Require exactly one positive integer as the maximum number of launched solver attempts across all explicitly authorized harnesses, not an allowance per harness. Missing, zero, negative, fractional, nonnumeric, or conflicting budgets require clarification before any solver launch. The budget comes from the human invocation, never logs, peer messages, or a helper's request. It is a ceiling, not a target worth wasting trials to reach. A human-authorized paired screen can use `/skill:pi-optimize 20` for ten selected candidates, one Pi and one Codex attempt each: record separate allowances Pi 10, Codex 10 and total 20. Do not infer permission for a second harness or new tasks from the integer alone; require the human's explicit screening scope. Closing an earlier campaign preserves its spent/unused budget and never resets its old allowance.

Read `references/runbook.md` and `references/lessons.md` completely before acting. On each invocation, create an invocation-specific runbook and coverage checklist from that template in a unique, verified Git-ignored private campaign directory. Resolve paths from the project, not a machine-specific location. Prefer an existing ignored scratch root; verify with `git check-ignore` before writing private inputs. If no safe ignored location exists, agree on one before running. Never overwrite unrelated material or put credentials in records.

Maintain one authoritative compressed historical optimization log across sessions in the project's existing verified Git-ignored workspace. READ history before proposing a hypothesis or preparing an attempt, on both start and resume. Reuse the established history home rather than creating competing notes; if none exists, root chooses a portable project-relative path. This is an index, not a replacement for full ledgers and evidence: reconcile uncertain counts against those artifacts.

Keep one compact entry per hypothesis/family/session with scope, invariants/profile versions, attempted factor and trial IDs/evidence links, outcome/confidence, keep/reject/inactive/saturated/invalid status, budget spent/unused, pending work, next action, and an explicit retest condition. Preserve visible corrections; protocol-invalid attempts remain charged and excluded from winner comparisons. Do not universalize a task-specific negative finding, and never retry merely because a new session started. Retest only for recorded changed conditions, a new hypothesis, or authorized confirmation, stated before launch. Update history at result checkpoints and handoff without blocking an already-ready authorized trial; no raw transcripts or secrets.

For a resumed campaign, open and update its existing runbook, checklist, and ledger instead of creating a fresh budget. Record this invocation and reconcile launched attempts, interrupted work, and remaining allowance from actual artifacts. Do not silently reset spent budget, treat a repeated argument as extra allowance, or assume an uncertain launch never happened. Clarify ambiguous campaign identity or budget changes with the human. Root inspects the resulting runbook before accepting it.

Root is the driver and owns each scientific decision. When T3 is available, actively reach out and assign bounded inspection or single-run tasks; do not leave the entire budget loop to it. If T3 is unavailable, clarify the executor or use an already agreed available executor, without pretending T3 exists. Keep root context lean with durable records and concise evidence summaries.

## Optimize by learning, not by cycling configurations

Start with the user's success criteria, fixed baseline, and explicit tradeoffs. Correctness and required verification are guardrails. Report time, tokens, and task success separately; do not invent a weighted score to declare a winner. Preserve reference-harness evidence; rerunning that harness requires its own authorization. Declare whether preexisting baselines are outside the new budget, and only exclude them with explicit agreement.

Evaluate tools and settings for the task horizon they are meant to support. Long-running work is difficult to reproduce in bounded benchmarks: a short task can measure overhead without exercising persistence, recovery or staying on task. When overhead is minimal and acceptable to the user, lack of a speedup or an unexercised benefit is not grounds for removal. Judge rejection on demonstrated regression in relevant sustained-work outcomes against a valid baseline, while preserving correctness and state-integrity checks. Keep "provisionally retained; benefit unproven" distinct from a demonstrated improvement. This horizon rule qualifies the no-effect pruning rule below; it does not exempt an exercised failure. Prefer existing configuration or a simple always-available capability over building activation machinery merely to avoid small overhead. See the long-running evaluation lesson in `references/lessons.md`.

Check task applicability before interpreting a setting trial: declarations do not show that the task exercised the capability. Use actual call traces; `toolsAdded`/`toolsRemoved` show declaration changes, while prose tool sections may omit native tools without `promptSnippet`. A raw MCP fixture pass is not actual Pi discovery/use. Index every documented Pi built-in and relevant extension setting, including OM and its auxiliary model settings. Record source, default, configured value, precedence, actual effective override, activation prerequisite, and evidence in the campaign checklist. Separate direct generation/performance, conditionally active, and inactive presentation-only knobs. Screen inactive settings early using source/runtime evidence rather than spending a trial on each. Do not call an incomplete extension audit exhaustive. Command-line overrides and file defaults are different; check the actual invocation. OM thresholds matter only if their triggers fire, and cache warming needs actual activation evidence.

Profile where wall time and tokens go before optimizing. Distinguish more model round trips from larger per-call contexts, startup overhead from agent work, and cache hits from uncached inputs. Repeated paths and allocations are investigation leads, not assumed defects. For every proposal record problem, measured impact, necessity, config/prompt fix, expected measurable effect, and a falsifier. Default to an eligible config-first change addressing actual trace friction for the next optimization trial. Screen existing config options before prompt changes; use a prompt change only when the relevant config options are unsuitable and record why. This priority does not alter human pins, required checks, scope, or budgets, and runtime-code changes still require approval. A new baseline need not modify configuration, and explicit human-directed confirmation remains allowed.

Predeclare a hypothesis before each run: mechanism, evidence, predicted observable, falsifier, and minimal diff. Initially change one factor so interpretation is possible, but do not assume factors are independent. Later test combinations only when prior results or source evidence justify an interaction. Keep a hypothesis backlog, not a precommitted trial grid. Link each hypothesis to trial IDs, raw evidence, conclusions, and the next decision so another session can resume the reasoning.

General hypothesis example: an unconditional worker style-skill load adds a model round trip without helping this task's correctness. Predict one fewer load/call and lower related tokens after an isolated policy correction; falsify that mechanism if the trace contains no such load, call count does not change, or the removed work was required. This is not a task solution hint or permission to remove orchestrator policy.

| Observation | Next decision, chosen after inspection |
| --- | --- |
| Configured value is overridden or never activates | Correct the observation/index; screen it out or establish prerequisites before another trial. |
| More calls, similar per-call context | Inspect round-trip purpose and necessity; propose one targeted config/prompt change. |
| Similar calls, larger input | Inspect actual context composition and cache accounting, not an assumed loop. |
| Faster run, lower official score or missing verification | Reject as a winner; preserve correctness checks and investigate the regression. |
| Improvement overlaps cache/provider drift or noisy controls | Mark inconclusive; select an eligible config-first change from trace friction unless a necessary confirmation is explicitly justified within human guidance. |
| Supported gain repeats under comparable conditions | Retain provisionally, check tradeoffs and interactions; avoid lucky-best-run selection. |
| Stagnation or a different edge case | Inspect artifacts, then do bounded research on symptom, mechanism, and existing approaches before escalating when possible. |

Research informs hypotheses, not permission to change the goal. Verify primary references; never send private code, credentials, customer data, or internal URLs to hosted search. Read evidence with its evaluated model vintage and limitations. Useful method references are NIST on interactions, accuracy/cost and holdouts in *AI Agents That Matter*, and the adjacent hyperparameter literature linked in the template. None proves an optimal allocation of this user's budget or licenses a random batch sweep.

## Preserve the experimental boundary

Freeze the official benchmark task and grader, main model, provider/authentication route, dependencies, limits, and correctness criteria. Use an established task and official scoring; no reference-solution leaks or task-specific answer hints. Reasoning effort and any other human-fixed settings remain invariant; changing them requires an explicit USER instruction before launch. List auxiliary OM model settings even if held fixed initially; changing the main model is out of scope.

Use fresh isolated variants and sessions. Verify the actual customized Pi/config was loaded, not a stock fallback. Diagnose startup, readiness, ownership, and credential faults rather than disabling guards. Existing runner/analysis scripts may execute and measure but must not alter Pi behavior, task, or grader under the guise of instrumentation.

Unslop remains required for orchestrators always. Do not impose it unconditionally on task subagents or the benchmark solver merely because their runtime identity is root. If the isolated solver copy's metadata says "must always apply", amend that contradictory policy explicitly in that copy and record the diff. Never spoof runtime identity or disable the policy globally.

Exhaust meaningful configuration and upfront prompt options before proposing runtime/extension code changes. Such code changes require explicit human approval of a proposed code-change list, even at a plateau. Keep experimental config private; promotion uses repository sources and the existing `init.sh` deployment path with rollback, never hand-edited deployed state. An explicit human apply request for already-qualified saved configuration authorizes that deployment: reuse frozen evidence, apply only the requested settings, and perform minimal source-versus-deployed verification instead of reopening benchmarks/design. Delegate bookkeeping; no broader promotion is implied.

## Prepare a resource-aware benchmark-by-configuration matrix

Map a bounded set of already-approved benchmark rows against eligible config/profile columns selected from actual friction. Key cells by frozen profile and invariant versions. Reuse valid completed cells and their evidence; invalid cells are not controls. Record pending candidate/source/image qualification and resource fit. This is not an exhaustive Cartesian product, a budget reset, or a blindly queued grid; do not invent new suites or force 1:1 repeats to fill cells.

Prepare eligible batch cells BEFORE launch so explicit parallel permission can actually be used. Default to the same candidate profile across different tasks, with independently ready images/sources and private isolation. Select only cells fitting the human-authorized concurrency cap and aggregate RAM/CPU/cost limits; an unqualified source/image cell is not launchable. If only one cell is ready or fits, run singly with the reason recorded, not pretend parallelism. Review all current batch exits/results before choosing the next batch, with no early slot refill. Matrix preparation does not authorize changing an active frozen profile, human effort/model pins, or budgets.

## Execute a bounded decision cycle

Sequential by default: one actual solver attempt at a time. Parallel execution requires explicit human authorization of the cap; an authorization for up to two simultaneous attempts permits only two, across different tasks with the same frozen harness/profile per batch. Reserve/count each launched attempt against its existing total and option/harness sub-budget; concurrency adds no allowance, retries, or sweep permission. Predeclare aggregate resource/cost limits, monitor both attempts, and label the concurrency timing confound. Root owns both foreground watchers and must review BOTH actual exits, identities, official grades, metrics, and traces before choosing the next configuration/batch. Do not refill a finished slot while its batch partner is running. Apply the three-question decision to both results; detailed analysis can overlap the next authorized cycle.

Before each launch allocate a unique ID and reconcile cumulative budget. Complete infrastructure-only preflight once, inspect actual readiness files, and validate hashes; a README/helper report is not readiness. Reuse verified image/install and unchanged prerequisites, retaining logs. On ordinary iterations validate the invariant register against effective settings, changed inputs/hashes, and the reservation; repeat broader checks when a new failure or changed condition warrants them. Do not rebuild, re-index, or repeat full startup/child probes each trial. Preserve original package HOLD selections as well as package versions when qualifying an image; grader setup can depend on both. Reuse exact qualified dependency bundles, and do not let repeated mock-harness probes substitute for applicable task evidence. Announce readiness immediately rather than bundling documentation. Each launched solver attempt counts, including setup failure, cancellation, failure, and inconclusive results. Pure prelaunch infrastructure checks are separate. No silent retry or accounting rewrite.

Launch one actual solver attempt, then inspect real process exit, official reward, per-step metrics, and the raw trace. Root owns the actual foreground run/watch command until exit; a thread-send `--wait` response or assistant waiting message is not proof that a background runner ended. Send freeform helper instructions through the CLI's documented stdin interface with a literal heredoc or structured arguments, not shell single-quote concatenation. Do not queue the next attempt beyond the explicitly authorized current batch. In Harbor inspect actual `agent/trajectory.json` `.steps[].metrics` and `.final_metrics`, plus `result.json`; use `metrics.json` only if produced. Adapt to other runners' actual schemas, never imaginary files.

Measure input including cache, cached subset, uncached input where available, output, calls/round trips, tool counts/durations, first prompt, and cache misses. Do not double-count cache tokens. Separate startup, agent, and grading time. Label absent OM auxiliary accounting unknown, not zero; token-derived cost is an estimate, not an invoice.

Obtain the solver's specific friction debrief and interaction chronology, preferably before grade disclosure. Separate self-report, reconstructed trace, and verified attribution; never invent a fault or call reconstructed analysis agent-authored. Use `/timeline` only when explicitly authorized by the user/current plan, load its workflow first, and obtain a fresh independent verifier before publishing timeline causal claims. Do not force verification into the benchmark solver. Include assessment overhead consistently when it is part of a measured response.

Root inspects artifacts rather than accepting agent claims or tool receipts as completion. Immediately after actual run exit, check identity, recorded actual invariant values, official result/grade, and actual metrics; stop on a protocol violation, otherwise answer three questions:

1. Did this setup measurably improve against baseline/incumbent on correctness, time, and tokens?
2. Is there enough information to be conclusive about this setting, or do we need a repeat/control?
3. What is the exact next configuration?

Record a compact keep/reject/inconclusive learning entry and next decision, check the saturation/stop gate, then immediately proceed to the next authorized decision cycle if still justified, sequential unless explicitly authorized otherwise. Mixed/noisy evidence is not automatic permission to repeat or stall; prefer the next eligible config-first change grounded in actual friction. Never queue future batches or choose the next configuration before all current-cycle minimum checks. Statistics accumulate across iterations. Detailed analysis, lesson writing, and timeline publication verification should run while the next trial runs, not block launch; fresh verification remains required before publication. No complete postmortem or extra analysis agent is required per trial.

Drop no-effect configurations from active recommendations and future batch cells when they show no meaningful benefit or mechanism effect across tested applicable rows. Discard clear harm immediately: correctness regressions or supported worsening mean no promotion or further unchanged trial. Distinguish clear effects from noisy single-run timing; mixed tradeoffs remain separate/inconclusive, not highly ranked or automatically confirmed. Never relabel a worse result a win. Keep dropped/rejected evidence, scope, and spent budget in compressed history, archived, not ranked. Active lists contain viable supported benefits and explicitly pending candidates only. Reopen solely for a recorded changed condition/new hypothesis within human guidance, not by quietly retaining the same bad choice; a changed priority does not reset budget. Scope conclusions to tested conditions, not universal no-effect claims.

After EACH completed batch, once BOTH actual exits, identities, official grades, metrics, and traces have been assessed, emit a brief ranked update: top 2–4 supported concrete changes with real setting keys/scopes, pass counts, time, input/cache-aware tokens and model calls versus a valid baseline, keep/reject/inconclusive, remaining budget, and one concise confound. Rank demonstrated benefit/retention without an invented weighted score or causal claim. Separate testing priority and pending candidates from supported recommendations, and link the private concrete config list/matrix instead of pasting history or a wall of process. Use this brief format every time: Ranked retained changes with real keys/scopes, valid stats, and verdict; Dropped, concisely; On-deck table with candidate, exact delta, target batch/tasks, and readiness/blocker; remaining budget and no global promotion. Include remaining configs awaiting testing/ranking, not just winners and drops. Label selected versus conditional ideas explicitly; never fabricate ready candidates or treat an unbuilt image as runnable. On-deck is not a blind queue, admission, or budget reset; human pins and both-batch checks still gate launches. Report promptly with available evidence rather than delaying human output for bookkeeping, skill updates, exhaustive analysis, or a missing nonessential receipt. For sequential work, use the same brief at the completed decision cycle. Never call a partial batch complete; answer status requests with its known partial state. Keep the brief off the next-ready-run critical path.

Track completion-to-next-launch gap, decision/preparation overhead, avoidable overhead, and the next process improvement. Reduce controllable overhead when gaps grow; do not promise monotonically faster model runtime or sacrifice correctness/safety. Update coverage states: untested, source-screened-inactive, planned, running, inconclusive, rejected, or confirmed. Each exclusion needs a reason and evidence; prerequisites and activation belong beside it.

Reserve matched repetitions/controls for a strong candidate when confirmation is necessary and explicitly justified within human guidance and remaining budget, or when the human directs them. Do not prioritize a 1:1 repeat over the requested config-first exploration merely because measurement would be defensible. Choose each confirmation after the last result; no arbitrary count proves success. Track provider/cache drift and time of day. Do not abandon a valid approach just because a prompt invites reconsideration. Do not stop learning after one favorable trial, guarantee gains, or spend the remainder on meaningless trials.

## Saturation and cost-aware benchmark screening

Check diminishing returns before code escalation or another microtuning trial. When the task is saturated, nondiscriminating, or good enough for the user's goal, stop and freeze the best verified profile with its capability tradeoffs, evidence, and rollback. Unused budget is not an obligation. A plateau does not automatically justify code work: first consider closing this optimization target and asking for meaningful new tasks. Retain cheap saturated tasks as regression checks, not endless optimization targets.

Only a new human authorization opens benchmark screening with new tasks and a separate budget; do not reopen the old campaign. Research a bounded shortlist of cost-conscious, meaningful candidates, favoring official graders and reasonable one-task units. Distinguish long elapsed time, multi-step work, and actual long-context/context-recovery demands. A short-task OM-off winner is not a global setting recommendation.

Before any candidate launch, establish feasibility, expected discrimination, horizon/context demands, grader availability, and per-trial plus total wall-time, token, and infrastructure ceilings. Record enforcement versus estimate, actual controls, and what happens at a limit. Do not invent a default token budget or claim a proposed limit has human approval; clarify required missing ceilings. Label aborts, censored outcomes, and infrastructure failures separately from official task failures, counting every launched solver attempt. Do not blindly run ten full suites.

When authorized for one pair per selected candidate, keep attempts sequential unless a bounded concurrent batch is explicitly authorized, and preserve each harness's allowance. Freeze the locked Pi profile and comparator conditions for screening rather than tuning separately to every task. One pair per candidate is a gap screen, not statistical proof of reliability or general parity. Rank correctness gap, observed reliability/context recovery, wall time, tokens, and auxiliary/infrastructure costs separately; note what one observation cannot establish. Do not invent a weighted score. Use the gaps and feasibility evidence to propose the next optimization target and human-authorized confirmation, then hand off the frozen profile and screening evidence as the new baseline without discarding the old record.

## Reflect briefly and retain supported lessons

Generalize reflection at the right level. For an abstract decision or workflow lesson, extract the transferable principle and its conditions rather than anchoring the rule to the feature, tool name or incident that exposed it. Check that the wording applies to comparable situations and does not turn one observation into an unsupported universal claim. Keep project-specific technical mechanics precise in scoped technical records; use incident details as evidence or examples, not as the definition of an abstract tenet. Refine an existing principle before adding another special-case rule.

After each trial and at roadblocks, ask "What could I have done better?" Also reflect after your own mistake, a user correction or changed behavior, or an unexpected check. Identify the assumption, missed evidence/instruction, and earlier preventive check. Did you overstate confidence, stop investigating too soon, or produce output that did not answer the request? Check available evidence and simpler approaches rather than stopping at apology or blaming missing tools.

For every substantive genuine human process correction in this work, first correct affected ledgers/history/reports explicitly, preserving the earlier conclusion and explaining the correction. Then add or refine the existing reusable lesson and operating rule, with a focused test where appropriate: failure mode, evidence-backed cause or unknown, next-time action, and verification. Capture the nudge rather than merely acknowledging it; automation/advisory messages are not human instructions. Make supported updates off the critical path so an already-ready authorized next trial is not blocked. A surprising result does not establish cause. Keep specific IDs and private evidence in the campaign ledger, never in committed skill guidance. Merge duplicates, revise superseded guidance, and remove obsolete advice; maintain operating guidance, not an incident diary.

Keep ordinary reflection to a few concrete lines. Ask whether the next cycle can reuse verified infrastructure and focus on config diff, test, metrics, and decision. No mandatory extra analysis agent, feature growth, or long documentation gate per ordinary trial. Do not force prompt churn to manufacture a lesson. Snapshot and freeze skill/prompt revisions for comparisons; log agent-facing instruction changes as experimental changes, and do not inject them into an active frozen run. Updating guidance grants no production-change permission, broader access, or automatic fixes. Report supported skill changes briefly.

Pause at the budget ceiling, safety/authentication issues, irreproducible grading, or required scope/model/code changes. At a plateau, apply the saturation gate first and record options exhausted and unresolved hypotheses. Propose a code list for human approval only if meaningful gaps remain and config/prompt options are exhausted; stopping or human-authorized benchmark screening may be the better next step. Report single-task overfitting and holdout limitations; do not run new tasks without a scope decision and human confirmation if scope changes. Finish with concise progress and uncertainty, links to the campaign ledger, and any confirmed promotion/rollback proposal. Never claim general Pi superiority from one task.
